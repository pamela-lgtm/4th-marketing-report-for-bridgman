The pace is the part that matters. The first 16 days produced 13 inquiries. The 15 days since have produced 7. Eight of the 20 inquiries turned into showings, a 40% rate, which tells us the listing itself is doing its job. Renters who see it want to tour it. What has dropped is the number of renters seeing it and reaching out in the first place.

Section Two

What changed since last week

Last week we recommended no change. Four showings in seven days and an application in hand told us the price was producing interest, and on that week's numbers, it was. Three things have changed since then.

The application did not lead to a second one

It remains the only application in 31 days. When a price is producing the right level of interest, a declined application is quickly followed by another qualified household. That has not happened.

Showing activity has slowed

The last showing was October 8. After building for three weeks, activity is not where it needs to be to give us a choice of residents. We are also now past the point where renters see the listing as new. Zillow shows every renter the days-on-market count, and at 31 days, many renters assume a listing has a problem and skip it.

The competition repriced around us

On October 6, 8001 Nemours Parkway cut its price to $4,200. On October 10, 7933 Dausset Street came on the market at $4,400. 13450 Gabor Avenue, a five-bedroom, has been asking $4,800 since September 25. A renter shopping Laureate Park today sees your property at the top of the list by a clear margin. Section Three shows each of these homes.

What the comparison tells us

Nothing in Laureate Park has leased above $5,000. That was true when we wrote our August analysis, and it is still true. The one $5,000 lease, 13273 Granger Avenue, was signed August 1, in the busiest part of the year, after a cut from $5,500.

Two of these homes rent their garage apartment to a separate tenant. At 13450 Gabor Avenue, the $4,800 rent is for the main house only, and the 910 sq ft apartment leased separately in September for $1,990. At 8033 Nemours Parkway, the main house leased for $4,525 and the apartment leased separately for $1,900. A renter considering those homes gets the main house and shares the property with another household.

This is where your investment property wins. At $4,950, a renter gets the entire property, including a private apartment no one else lives in, for $150 more than Gabor's main house alone. At $5,150, that gap is $350, and the comparison is much harder to win.

We have set 8736 Crick Alley aside. It leased as-is, with older finishes, and its listing history shows a 2,212 sq ft main house alongside a separate apartment, so its square footage likely counts both. It is a lower tier of home than yours.

The current price of $5,150 is essentially the rent the single best lease of the summer supports. That lease happened at the peak of the season. We are now in mid-October, competing against homes priced to today's market, and today's market indicates about $4,700.

Does the math point lower than $4,950?

Honestly, yes. Today's competition points closer to $4,700. We are recommending $4,950 first, rather than going straight there, for three reasons. Your property has already drawn eight showings and an application at a higher price, so renters are responding to it. $4,950 brings the rent under $5,000, which matters because a renter who sets $5,000 as the maximum in a search does not see the property at $5,150 at all. And $4,950 still leaves room for one more clear step if the market asks for it.

If the first five days at $4,950 do not produce enough interest for us to choose a well-qualified resident, the next step is $4,750, which lines up with today's competition. That is a step, not a floor. We will keep measuring and keep reacting to what the market tells us.

How this fits with our August analysis

Our August 11 analysis recommended $5,250 and set out a faster option at $4,950, just under the $5,000 that 8771 Betzig Alley leased for. It also set a checkpoint: if showing traffic did not come, we would move to $4,950.

Showing traffic did come, so on October 2 we took a smaller step to $5,150 rather than going straight to the checkpoint. With activity now slowing, moving to $4,950 completes the plan we set out at the start.

The rest of the August analysis still holds. The main residence is 2,880 sq ft and the apartment 687 sq ft. The apartment adds a feature premium of about $675, not a second rent. And no Laureate Park home has leased above $5,000 this year. The August numbers were right on the data available then. The market has moved since, and this report reflects that.
